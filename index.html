How to read this pattern
  1. Three bumps: a shoulder, a taller head, then a second shoulder about the height of the first.
  2. The line joining the two dips between the bumps is the neckline. A bearish pattern forms at the top of a rise; a bullish (inverse) one forms upside down at the bottom of a fall.
  3. It is a reversal pattern. It needs a trend to reverse. A bearish shape that appears after a long fall has nothing to reverse, so it is not a setup.
  4. Nothing happens until a candle closes through the neckline. That close is the entry. Bearish: sell short below it. Bullish: buy above it.
  5. Target: measure head to neckline, then project that distance from the neckline in the direction of the break.
  6. Stop loss: just past the second shoulder. If price comes back there, the pattern has failed. Get out with a small loss.
  7. Risk reward ratio: distance to the stop loss versus distance to the target. Minimum 1 : 2 here; 1 : 3 is better.
